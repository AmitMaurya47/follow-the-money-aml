# Circular flows and round-tripping

Circular flows return funds to their origin through a loop of accounts. They create a false appearance of commercial activity and can disguise the true owner.

## Cycles in the transaction graph
An account that sits inside a strongly connected component of two or more accounts takes part in a loop of transfers. Short cycles (two to four hops) that return most of the value to the origin are the strongest signal. Long cycles are weaker because they can occur in ordinary supply chains.

## Reciprocity
Heavy two-way traffic between the same pair of accounts, where money goes back and forth with little net change, can indicate round-tripping or wash trading. A high share of outgoing partners that also send money back is a signal to review.

## Investigation steps
Confirm the business purpose of the loop, check whether the accounts share owners or addresses, and compare the value returned to the origin with the value sent.
