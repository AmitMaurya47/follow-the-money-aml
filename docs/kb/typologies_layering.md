# Layering and pass-through typologies

Layering moves funds through several accounts to obscure their origin. Accounts used this way often show inbound and outbound totals that nearly match.

## Pass-through accounts
An account receives funds and forwards almost the same amount within a short period, keeping little. A high in/out balance ratio combined with a short holding time is the classic signal. Legitimate businesses also move money, so the signal is stronger when the account is new, has little history, or has an unexplained source of funds.

## Rapid forwarding
Inbound transfers passed on within 24 hours at a similar amount suggest a relay role. Look for repeated forwarding across many inbound events rather than one isolated case, and check whether the forwarded amounts go to new counterparties each time.

## Cross-border and multi-hop chains
Chains of transfers across several institutions, currencies, or payment formats make tracing harder. A chain that ends at a single beneficiary after several intermediate hops is a strong layering pattern and should be escalated for review.
